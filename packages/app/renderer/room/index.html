This is the Room.

Your agents work; you hear the one line that matters, and the board shows what words can't carry. Three things to set up, two minutes, all of them here again later.

  1. Pick a voice. Piper is bundled and speaks now. ElevenLabs is the best, with your own key.
  2. Connect Claude Code. Hooks, an output style so Claude writes its own spoken line, and the board.
  3. Turn on listening if you want to answer by voice: "hey kikoe, what's it doing".

What you can say

  • hey kikoe, what's it doing · did the tests pass · how long has it been going · what's waiting on me
  • kikoe, stop · go verbose · go silent · repeat that · switch to storefront
  • kikoe, clear the board · show me the board · back to the room
  • a bare yes, no, or apply when it has asked you something
  • with a mind of its own: anything. kik, tell it to add a retry to the fetch · what do you think of that diff · how long has this been going. After an exchange, the next thing you say needs no name.

Only what is addressed to it is kept, and shown in the Control Room under "what you said". Anything else is dropped the moment it is transcribed; a word count is all that is logged. The model loads in under a second and unloads after ten quiet minutes.

The board

        
The agent's whiteboard. It pins what can't be said: a diff to approve, a failing test's lines, a table, a picture, a page, an SVG it drew, or a diagram written as boxes and arrows. Each repo has a frame; drag to look around, pinch or control-scroll to zoom; drag a card to arrange. Nothing on it is editable. Pins fade after fifteen minutes.

        
From a shell, anything can pin: curl -K ~/.kikoe/show.curlrc -H "X-Kikoe-Kind: diff" --data-binary @changes.diff. Add -H "X-Kikoe-Ask: apply,no" and the command waits for your answer.

        

      


      

        
The walkie-talkie

        
Your phone as a window onto the canvas, and a microphone. Open the link on it and you get this Room — the same cards, live — with a button at the bottom: hold it, talk, let go. What you said reaches Kik the same way the headset does, the answer is spoken from this machine, and the work lands on both screens. Pinch to zoom, drag to look around; you can type to Kik too.

        
The phone watches and talks; it cannot approve a tool call, move a card or clear the board. Pages on localhost show on the desk only, since the phone's localhost is the phone. The button alone, without the canvas, is at /talk.

        
This is the one part of Kikoe that listens on the network rather than on this machine alone, so it is off until you turn it on and the socket closes again the moment you turn it off. A browser will not hand a microphone to a plain http page, so Kikoe serves the page over HTTPS with a certificate it signs itself — your phone will say the connection is not private, once. That warning is the price of your voice not going through anyone else's tunnel; the audio goes straight to this machine and is transcribed here.

        
If your phone still refuses the microphone after you continue past the warning, open /cert.pem on the same link, install it, and trust it in your phone's settings — then there is no warning and no doubt.

        
        
        

        

      


      

        
Limits

        
How much of each coding assistant's limit is gone, as a ring on the island. Hover a ring for its windows and when they reset. Kik can be asked out loud, too — "how much have I got left?"

        
Kikoe never signs in anywhere. Every reading is borrowed from a credential a tool on this machine already holds, so a tool you use appears on its own; one you don't, never does. Switching a row off stops its credential being read at all; it does not sign you out of the tool that owns the account. The idea and the design are Codenotch's.

        
        

        
 

        
No vendor publishes a clean "your limit is N% used" API for any of these. Each reads whatever the owning app itself reads, and those can change without notice — so every failure shows as a status with a date on it rather than an invented number.
